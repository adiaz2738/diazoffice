*Last checked: October 2026. Every home is different, and what's worth doing depends on the home, the price range, and the market. Talk with your agent before you spend money on anything.*

How your home looks when it hits the market affects how many buyers come to see it, how they feel when they walk in, and what they're willing to offer. You don't need to remodel to sell well. Most of what makes a difference is simple, and a lot of it costs more time than money.

This chapter covers what's usually worth doing, what usually isn't, and a few local rules to keep in mind before you start any work.

## Start with a plan

Before you call a contractor or buy a can of paint, walk through the home with your agent. At our first meeting, I'll point out what I think buyers will notice, good and bad, and we'll talk about what's worth doing for your home and your price range.

The goal isn't to make the home perfect. It's to remove the things that would make a buyer hesitate, and to show off what's already great about it.

## What's usually worth doing

- **Decluttering and depersonalizing.** Buyers need to picture their own life in the home. Clear off counters, thin out closets, and pack away personal photos and collections. If you're moving anyway, you'll have to pack eventually, so it helps to start now.
- **Deep cleaning.** Windows, floors, kitchens, and bathrooms especially. A clean home tells buyers it's been cared for.
- **Small repairs.** Leaky faucets, running toilets, sticking doors, burned-out bulbs, cracked outlet covers, and holes in the walls. Small issues add up in a buyer's mind and can make them wonder what else hasn't been taken care of.
- **Curb appeal.** The front of the home is the first thing buyers see, in person and online. Trim the plants, clean up the yard, sweep the walkway, and make sure the front door and house numbers look good.
- **Lighting.** Open the blinds, replace dim bulbs, and make the home as bright as you can.
- **Smells.** Pets, cooking, smoke, and musty rooms all stand out to buyers. Near the coast, damp or musty smells can be common in older homes, so airing the home out and dealing with any mildew helps.

### Making the home feel like theirs

A home that reflects your personality is a great place to live. When you sell, though, the goal changes. Bold paint colors, very specific decor, and big collections, like a wall of mounted hunting trophies or a room built around one hobby, can pull buyers' attention toward your taste instead of how they'd use the space. Some buyers will love it, but many will spend the showing reacting to the decor instead of picturing themselves there.

You don't have to strip the home bare. Toning it down to something more neutral simply lets more buyers see themselves living there. How much this matters depends on the home, the buyers in your area, and the price. A very well-priced home will sell either way, but in most cases, a more neutral home helps.

And sometimes it just isn't practical. Maybe there's a room full of Legos, a collection that's hard to move, or you're still living there with a busy family. That's okay. Do what you reasonably can, and we'll work with the rest. It's not a reason to put off selling.

### Light cosmetic updates

Some updates are often worth it when something is clearly worn out:

- **Flooring.** Stained or worn carpet can drag down a whole house. Replacing it with new carpet, or with laminate or luxury vinyl plank, which a lot of buyers prefer these days, especially those with pets, can make a big difference for the cost.
- **Paint.** Fresh, neutral paint on scuffed or very bold walls.
- **Fixtures.** Dated or broken light fixtures and hardware, where replacing them is simple.

The key is that the update is done well. Buyers can tell the difference between a home that's been refreshed and one that's been covered up.

## What usually isn't worth doing

**Big remodels right before selling** usually don't pay for themselves. A new kitchen or bathroom costs a lot, takes time, and buyers may not even like your choices. It can also delay your listing, and permitted work can take longer than you expect.

**Covering things up.** Quick, cheap fixes meant to make a home look better than it is tend to backfire. Painting over old cabinets, doorknobs, light switches, or even plumbing, or rushing a low-quality remodel, usually shows. It can make buyers wonder what else has been covered up, and their inspector will look closely. This comes up a lot with homes that were rentals, sat vacant, or were inherited. In most cases, a clean, honest home in its real condition does better than one that's been dressed up.

Sometimes a real repair does make sense before listing, like fixing a problem that would scare off most buyers or keep them from getting a loan. That's different from covering something up: it's done properly, by licensed contractors where required, and disclosed. We'll talk through those case by case. For most homeowners, though, making the home clean and presentable, with light cosmetic updates where they're needed, is the best approach. When the home needs more work than you want to take on, it's often better to price it for its condition, as covered in [Chapter 3](/sellers-guide/pricing-your-home).

## Before you start any work: local rules

A few rules here can turn a simple project into a problem if you don't know about them:

- **Permits.** Work that normally requires a permit still requires one when you're selling. Unpermitted work tends to come up in city inspection reports, as covered in [Chapter 5](/sellers-guide/disclosures-and-local-requirements), and it's something you'd have to disclose. Use licensed contractors, and keep invoices and receipts. Buyers will often ask for them.
- **Trees.** Monterey, Pacific Grove, and Carmel-by-the-Sea all regulate tree removal, and some significant pruning, on private property. Don't remove a tree to improve the view or the curb appeal without checking with the city first. See [Chapter 6 of the Buyer's Guide](/buyers-guide/water-permits-local-rules) for more on tree rules.
- **Defensible space.** If your home is in a High or Very High fire hazard zone, state law generally requires documentation of a defensible space inspection when you sell, or an agreement that the buyer will get one, as covered in [Chapter 5](/sellers-guide/disclosures-and-local-requirements). Clearing vegetation around the home before that inspection helps, and it looks better too.
- **Water fixtures.** On the Peninsula, you'll likely need to meet the water district's fixture requirements before closing. If you're already replacing a toilet or faucet, buy one that meets the requirements.

## Staging

Staging means arranging furniture and decor so the home shows at its best. It can make rooms feel bigger and help the home stand out in photos.

Most importantly, it helps buyers picture themselves living there. A lot of buyers have a hard time imagining how they'd use a space. In an empty or awkward room, they start calculating: "Would our couch fit here? What would we even do with this room?" In a well-staged room, they're thinking, "That's a nice space," and getting ideas. Staging does some of the imagining for them.

- **If you're still living there,** staging usually means editing what you have: removing extra furniture, depersonalizing, rearranging, and adding a few touches.
- **If the home is vacant,** like many inherited homes, empty rooms can feel smaller and colder than they are. Professional staging can help, especially in the main living areas.

Whether it's worth the cost depends on the home and the price range. Staging is paid for by the seller, and we have stagers we work with regularly and can recommend. We'll talk through whether it makes sense for your home.

### Virtual staging

Virtual staging adds furniture to photos digitally instead of physically. It costs less, but since January 1, 2026, California law requires that any listing photo that's been altered to add, remove, or change physical features be clearly labeled as altered, with the original photo available to buyers. Basic edits like brightness, color, and cropping don't count.

Virtual staging can still be useful, especially for vacant homes. Just know that buyers will see both versions, and the home has to live up to the photos when they walk in.

## Photos and marketing

Most buyers find homes online and see your home there before they see it in person, so the photos matter a lot. Our team provides professional photos and a 3D tour, and drone photos and video are available when they make sense for the home. The work you do to prepare the home pays off most here.

A 3D tour lets buyers walk through the home online. It's especially helpful when showings are limited, like when there are tenants in the home or the owner can't easily leave for showings, and for buyers moving from out of the area.

The day before the photos, go room by room: clear the counters, hide cords and trash cans, make the beds, put away toiletries, and move cars out of the driveway. The 10 things you love most about your home from our first meeting can help us decide what to highlight.

## Getting ready for showings

Once you're on the market, buyers and their agents will come through, sometimes on short notice. A few things help:

- **Keep it show-ready.** A quick daily reset of the kitchen, bathrooms, and beds goes a long way.
- **Be flexible.** Buyers who can't get in tend to move on to the next house.
- **Leave during showings.** Buyers relax and take their time when the seller isn't there.
- **Secure valuables and private items.** Jewelry, cash, medications, important papers, and anything else personal should be locked up or taken with you.
- **Plan for pets.** Take them with you if you can, or keep them secured, and let your agent know they're there.

## If you live out of the area

Many sellers here, especially second-home owners and heirs, are preparing a home from far away. That's very doable. ## Where to focus your own research

- **Before you spend money:** Walk through the home with your agent and agree on what's worth doing.
- **Before any project:** Check whether it needs a permit, and whether any trees are involved.
- **The week before photos:** Declutter, clean, and handle the small repairs.
